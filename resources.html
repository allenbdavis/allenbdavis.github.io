Teaching Resources

Over the years I've built a collection of interactive web tools for use in my physics and astronomy classes. Some are games, some are simulations, and some are practical utilities. All of them grew out of a genuine classroom need — a concept I wanted students to explore more deeply, or a task that I thought technology could make more engaging. I'm sharing them here in case they're useful to other teachers or curious learners.

IB Physics Test Grader

📚 IB Physics (SL & HL) 🎯 Grades 11–12 🤖 AI-powered

Writing good responses to IB Physics questions is a skill that takes practice — and students benefit enormously from fast, specific feedback. This tool uses AI to evaluate student responses to IB-style exam questions against the kinds of criteria used in real IB mark schemes. Students can submit a question, their answer, and receive detailed, criterion-level feedback on what they got right, what they missed, and how they might improve.

This tool is intended as a study aid and a feedback accelerator, not a replacement for teacher assessment. It works best when students use it to iterate on their responses before an exam, not just to check a final answer.

Physh Bowl

📚 IB Physics (SL & HL) 🎯 Grades 11–12 🎮 Game

Physh Bowl is a quiz bowl–style game designed to make physics review fast, fun, and a little competitive. Players race against a timer to answer multiple-choice and short-answer questions drawn from across the IB Physics curriculum — mechanics, waves, electricity, fields, and beyond.

I originally built this as a class warm-up activity and end-of-unit review game, but students quickly started playing it on their own before exams. It works well for individual practice or for projecting in class as a team competition. The name is a terrible pun. I regret nothing.

TESS Planet Hunting Simulation

🔭 Astronomy / Astrophysics 🎯 Grades 9–12 🌍 Simulation

This simulation puts students in the role of a NASA scientist working with data from the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS). Students examine light curves — graphs of a star's brightness over time — and look for the subtle, periodic dips that betray the presence of an orbiting planet passing in front of its host star.

The activity is grounded in the same transit photometry technique that real planet hunters use, and it connects directly to my own doctoral research confirming TESS exoplanet candidates. Students come away with a genuine feel for how fragile and exciting the evidence for a new world actually is.

Battle Dice

📊 Probability & Statistics 🎯 Grades 7–12 🎮 Game

Battle Dice is a strategy game built around probability. Players design custom dice — choosing how to distribute the numbers on each face — and then pit them against an opponent's dice in repeated head-to-head rolls. The catch: dice matchups can be non-transitive. Die A might beat Die B, and Die B might beat Die C, but Die C can still beat Die A. This counterintuitive result is a great entry point for discussions about expected value, distributions, and the difference between short-run and long-run outcomes.

I use this game as both an introduction to probability concepts and as a conversation starter about how statistics can mislead intuition. It tends to generate a lot of genuine surprise — which is exactly what I'm after.
